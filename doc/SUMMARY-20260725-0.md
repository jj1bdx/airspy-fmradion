# Summary of changes: 20260716-0 to 20260725-0

This document summarizes all changes between tag `20260716-0` and
commit `c43def2` (released as `20260725-0`): 55 commits, 45 files
changed, +7471/−330 lines.

The line counts are dominated by documentation: of the 45 files, 33
are new files under `doc/` (analysis documents, their figures, and
four measurement scripts). The source change is small and net
*negative* — 10 files under `include/`, `sfmbase/` and `main.cpp`,
+249/−319 lines — because two of the four source threads delete more
than they add. This release consists of four threads of work: (a) an
analysis, retune and cleanup of the FM stereo pilot PLL, which found
the loop mildly under-damped, moved it to zeta ~= 0.71, replaced the
GNU Radio `fast_atan2f()` phase detector with `std::atan2()`, and
shortened the stereo lock decision from 0.5 s to 0.2 s; (b) a review,
speed rewrite and behavioral fix of the FM multipath filter, together
with the first ground-truth test harness it has ever had; (c) a
rewrite of the two FIR low-pass filters onto single VOLK dot products;
and (d) two independent latency measurement campaigns, the second of
which measures the receiver's end-to-end delay against an analog radio
for the first time. Only one change alters steady-state decoded audio:
the multipath filter's adaptation-rate scaling above `-E 36`.

## 1. FM stereo pilot PLL: analysis, retune, and cleanup

Four sub-threads, all touching `PilotPhaseLock`, and all backed by
measurement documents.

### 1.1 The analysis that started it

[doc/PLL_ANALYSIS_20260722.md](PLL_ANALYSIS_20260722.md) (`9d45096`
and five follow-up commits) models the loop exactly — a 5-state linear
model of the phase detector, the in-loop phasor biquad LPF, the
first-order PI section and the NCO accumulator — and verifies the model
against the compiled binary on real IQ (`8a15ba5`). The finding: the
loop's dominant closed-loop pole pair is damped at **zeta ~= 0.57**,
mildly under-damped, and the reason is not the PI gains but the in-loop
phasor low-pass filter, whose poles dominate the loop dynamics. The
document also covers stereo separation (`99da4fc`) and ends with an
executive summary and a conclusion (`7a93876`, `1cd146e`). `b77f6e2`
corrects the block diagram, which had drawn `fast_atan2` taking only
one input.

### 1.2 `std::atan2()` replaces `fast_atan2f()`

The phase detector used `Utility::fast_atan2f()`, GNU Radio's 257-entry
atan table with linear interpolation, on inputs that were already
`double`.
[doc/CORE_MATH_ATAN2F_20260722.md](CORE_MATH_ATAN2F_20260722.md)
(`8cd9171`) and
[doc/STD_ATAN2_X86_64_20260722.md](STD_ATAN2_X86_64_20260722.md)
(`a7e381a`, `56c1067`, `aae2153`, `910138a`) measure the alternatives on
both architectures. `8dbf8cc` makes the swap: the table lookup and its
narrowing to `float` are gone, and the phase error is now correctly
rounded. Per-call cost is platform dependent — faster on arm64/macOS,
about 2x slower on x86_64/glibc — but it is a negligible fraction of
one core at 384 kHz, and the decoded output is unchanged. `6c29ecf`
corrects the source comment, which had claimed a speed win on all
platforms.

The cleanup followed in `3a07110` and `8a4d90f`: `fast_atan2f()` and
its GNU Radio license block, about 173 lines, are removed from
`include/Utility.h`, which `PilotPhaseLock.cpp` no longer includes at
all (swapped for `<algorithm>` and `<cmath>`). This single deletion is
most of the release's negative source line count.

### 1.3 The zeta ~= 0.71 retune

`a3faf66`, documented in
[doc/PLL_REDESIGN_20260723.md](PLL_REDESIGN_20260723.md), moves the
dominant pole pair into the conventional 0.70-0.73 damping band while
holding the loop natural frequency and the loop structure constant. It
is a two-knob change, both in `PilotPhaseLock`'s constructor
initializer list:

- The in-loop phasor biquad LPF is **widened**, real corners ~34/160 Hz
  → ~40/188 Hz: coefficients `(1.46974784e-06, 0, 0, -1.99682419,
  0.996825659)` → `(2.037743564e-06, 0, 0, -1.996259818, 0.996261856)`
  for both `m_biquad_phasor_i1` and `m_biquad_phasor_q1`. Unity DC gain
  is preserved and the 38 kHz image is still suppressed by ~105 dB.
- The first-order PI section's gains are **rescaled by x0.889** to hold
  the natural frequency where it was: `(0.000304341788,
  -0.000304324564, 0)` → `(2.705503620719e-04, -2.705350504729e-04,
  0)`.

Verification, against the exact 5-state model and on
`test-files/piano_iqtest.wav`: zeta = 0.7101, fn = 22.34 Hz, stable;
steady-state tracking, jitter and pilot level unchanged (19000.012 Hz,
std 0.045 Hz); the acquisition first swing shrinks from −2.66 Hz to
−1.52 Hz; decoded audio identical to −90 dBFS. Open-loop margins
improve from **PM 46.2° / GM 19.3 dB** (the released `main` loop) to
**PM 51.6° / GM 21.9 dB**, measured in
[doc/PLL_ANALYSIS_3_20260724.md](PLL_ANALYSIS_3_20260724.md) (`8f1354b`)
and [doc/PLL_ANALYSIS_2_20260723.md](PLL_ANALYSIS_2_20260723.md)
(`a94f0bb`, `e3f5f1d`) respectively.

Two further retunes were designed, measured, and then **reverted**:
[doc/PLL_EXPERIMENT_20260724.md](PLL_EXPERIMENT_20260724.md)
(`9f4d431`, zeta ~= 1.00 by widening the biquad further — better
margins and acquisition, worse jitter) and
[doc/PLL_EXPERIMENT_2_20260724.md](PLL_EXPERIMENT_2_20260724.md)
(`dcbc16e`, halving the PI zero at constant bandwidth). Both documents
ship; neither change is in the source. The second one also establishes
a methodological point worth keeping: with the biquad frozen, zeta is
pinned at 0.71 and stops being a useful proxy for ringing.

### 1.4 Lock time and small cleanups

- `ed5ff93` shortens the lock decision (settling) time from **0.5 s to
  0.2 s**: `m_lock_delay` is `6.0 / bandwidth_pll` instead of `15.0 /
  bandwidth_pll`, i.e. 76800 instead of 192000 samples at 384 kHz.
  Stereo comes up sooner after a tune or a fade.
- `753ed0d` retypes `pilot_frequency`, `m_lock_delay`, `m_lock_cnt` and
  `m_pilot_periods` from `int` to `unsigned int`, and renames the public
  `static constexpr double bandwidth` to `bandwidth_pll`.
- `5fdff77` deletes `m_biquad_phasor_i2`/`_q2`, a second phasor biquad
  stage that was constructed but never used.

## 2. FM multipath filter: review, rewrite, and the first ground truth

`MultipathFilter` is a blind CMA/Godard equalizer with NLMS step
normalization, enabled by `-E stages`. It had never been tested against
a channel whose true impulse response was known.
[doc/MULTIPATH_FILTER_DESIGN_20260724.md](MULTIPATH_FILTER_DESIGN_20260724.md)
(`65d875e`, `89eb6ea`, `7f30c95`, `fd694f1`) is the review; section 0
lists the resulting diff and section 17 the ground-truth results.

**The harness** (`ac1c697`, extended by `7707a64`):
[doc/make_two_ray_channel.py](make_two_ray_channel.py) synthesizes a
two-ray channel — static or with a time-varying fade — over a clean IQ
recording, and [doc/eval_two_ray_snr.py](eval_two_ray_snr.py) scores a
decode of it against the decode of the clean original. This is what
made every number below falsifiable.

**Speed** (`0f094d8`): six of the eleven O(N) passes per four input
samples are gone. The delay line is now a ring buffer storing each
sample twice, so the newest N samples are always contiguous for VOLK
and the per-sample `m_state.erase(m_state.begin())` shift disappears;
the NLMS power sum is maintained incrementally instead of being
recomputed into a heap-allocated scratch buffer on every update, with
exactness restored every 65536 updates. Measured **1.6x faster** from
`-E36` through `-E200`, with decoded audio matching the old code to
−104 dB.

**The one on-air change** (`0f094d8`): the NLMS `alpha` is now scaled
with the filter order and clamped at 0.5, so the per-tap adaptation
rate `mu = alpha / sum(|state|^2)` stays at its `-E36` value instead of
falling as 1/N as `-E` is raised. `-E36` is unchanged by construction;
at `-E100` this is worth **+6.1 dB** of post-discriminator SNR.

**Robustness** (`0f094d8`): `FmDecoder::process` now calls
`reset_state()` alongside `initialize_coefficients()` on a divergence
reset — previously the non-finite sample that triggered the reset
stayed in the delay line and caused a burst of further resets — and
`process()` bounds the output and error magnitude at 10 rather than
only rejecting non-finite values, written as "not within the limit" so
that NaN still trips it.

**Guidance** (`0f094d8`, `2bf89b7`, `37bec3c`): the measured result that
matters most to users is that **more stages is not safer**. Taps beyond
the echo delay spread carry no signal, add gradient noise, and slow
adaptation: on a synthesized 3 microsecond echo, `-E200` decodes
**6.9 dB worse than switching the filter off**, while the optimum for
that channel is near `-E12`; for an 8 microsecond echo the optimum
moves to `-E36`. On shallow or fading channels the filter is a net loss
at any setting — which is the mechanism behind the long-standing "turn
off if reception becomes unstable" caution. The `-E` help text
(`main.cpp`) gains a "Size this to the echo delay spread" line, the
long explanation moves from `main.cpp` to `MultipathFilter.cpp` where
it belongs, and `README.md` gets the sizing advice.

Interface tidying in the same thread: the unreachable
`get_reference_level()` is deleted and the coefficient/error accessors
in `FmDecode.h` become `const`.

## 3. FIR low-pass filters rewritten on VOLK dot products

`9de5688`, documented in [doc/LPF_VOLK_20260725.md](LPF_VOLK_20260725.md).

`LowPassFilterFirIQ` and `LowPassFilterFirAudio` (`sfmbase/Filter.cpp`)
now compute each output as a single VOLK dot product. `process()`
builds one contiguous `[state | input]` history buffer per block in a
new `volk::vector` scratch member, and emits each output with one dot
product over all `order + 1` taps: `volk_32fc_32f_dot_prod_32fc` for
the IQ path (complex samples against real taps) and
`volk_32f_x2_dot_prod_32f` for the audio path. Because the coefficients
are real-valued, the IQ path needs no complex coefficient vector.
Coefficients are stored **reversed** once at construction
(`m_coeff_reversed`), so the dot product is a true convolution.

What this deletes is the interesting part: the hand-rolled warm-up
region, the linear-phase symmetry fold, and the `n < order` /
`n >= order` state-update branch, which collapses to a single
`std::copy`. Net −11 lines across `Filter.cpp` and `Filter.h`.

The rewrite also fixed a **latent bug**: the old warm-up loops summed
taps `j = 1 .. order` and therefore dropped the `coeff[0]` tap on the
first `order` outputs of every block. It was nearly harmless in
practice because every shipped coefficient table is a tapered windowed
sinc whose edge tap is at most −67 dB (exactly zero for the 3-tap IF
filter of the default and wide FM presets), which is why it survived
undetected.

Measured: decoded audio matches the previous code to about **134 dB**
signal-to-difference — the float32 re-association floor, i.e. the two
implementations differ only in summation order — and whole-pipeline
user CPU drops about **8 percent**.

## 4. Output latency: two measurement campaigns

### 4.1 Sampled PortAudio output latency

[doc/LATENCY_CHECK_20260719.md](LATENCY_CHECK_20260719.md), built over
nine commits (`bf2b91a` through `d3f1aa1`), measures the *actual*
output-stage latency delivered by the binary — decoder block pull to
audio sampled at the output device — rather than trusting requested or
reported values. Ten `-L` points on a virtual loopback device, plus the
physical FiiO K7.

Its main results: the CoreAudio grant follows **granted = B + R**, where
R is the request in frames and B the smallest power of two ≥ 3R, so a
40 ms request is granted 210.667 ms and one requested millisecond can
cost 22-86 ms of buffer at a bucket cliff; delivered latency is a ring
buffer *fill level*, not a capacity, and moves with history; and — the
finding that invalidated the day's earlier absolute numbers — Rogue
Amoeba SoundSource's ACE component was silently adding ~29 ms to the
loopback path and ~44 ms to the K7 chain, entirely invisible to
PortAudio's reported latency. Sections 15 and 16 re-measure the whole
ladder with SoundSource quit; section 17 consolidates the SoundSource
findings.

### 4.2 End-to-end delay against a reference receiver

[doc/LATENCY_MEASUREMENT_20260725.md](LATENCY_MEASUREMENT_20260725.md)
(`02d311c`, `8ac0060`) measures the complete delay for the first time —
antenna to analog DAC output — by recording an analog FM radio tuned to
the same broadcast on one channel and the airspy-fmradion output on the
other, so that the entire capture path cancels in the lag between them.
Two scripts ship with it: [doc/measure_lr_delay.py](measure_lr_delay.py)
tracks the lag, and [doc/loopback_clock_cal.py](loopback_clock_cal.py)
calibrates the capture clock against the DAC via an analog loopback.

| `-L` | Granted by CoreAudio | Measured end-to-end delay |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 13.667 ms | 34 → 46 ms, never settles |
| 5 | 26.333 ms | 37.37 ms |
| 10 | 52.667 ms | 54.36 ms |
| 20 | 105.333 ms | 91.63 ms |
| 30 | 200.667 ms | 132.59 ms |
| 40 = default | 210.667 ms | 151.15 ms |

Key results: the output stage is nearly the whole delay, with the
Airspy HF+ and the entire decode chain contributing only 6-7 ms and the
USB DAC at most 1.32 ms; on a live SDR the deep output buffers **never
fill**, so `-L ≥ 20` delivers well below the granted capacity, the
opposite of the FileSource-driven ladder in section 4.1; `-L 3` has no
steady state, growing about 1 ms/s; and the delay creeps upward by
about 13 ms per hour because nothing disciplines the SDR sample clock
against the DAC clock. The clock calibration (Rubix24 at −189.54 ppm,
FiiO K7 at −7.33 ppm) was cross-checked by two independent estimators
agreeing to 0.026 ppm; it changes the delay figures by at most 0.03 ms,
which is itself the useful result — it is now known rather than
assumed. Each `-L` was recorded once, so the deep-`-L` figures are one
observed fill state rather than an expected value; this is stated as
the dominant uncertainty in the document.

`8ac0060` adds an "Output latency and the `-L` option" section to
`README.md` with the ladder, the `-L 10` recommendation, and the
warning against `-L 3` and below.
